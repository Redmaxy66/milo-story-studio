# Milo Continuity Rules

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Approved derived continuity rules
**Source authority:** Milo Character Bible v1.0

## Purpose

These rules define how Milo stories are checked for continuity against approved canon.

They do not create new canon.

If these rules conflict with an approved Character Bible source file, the approved source file wins.

Approved source files:

- `MILO_CHARACTER.md`
- `MILO_BACKSTORY.md`
- `PERSONALITY_RULES.md`
- `VOICE_GUIDE.md`
- `VISUAL_REFERENCE.md`

## 1. Character identity

Milo Moonberry must remain:

- a lovable young magical forest explorer and storyteller
- resident of Moonberry Wood
- warm and trusted in tone
- appropriate for children aged 5–10

Milo should remain consistent with these core traits:

- cheerful
- curious
- kind
- brave
- gently mischievous
- compassionate
- optimistic
- emotionally safe
- encouraging
- playful
- interested in the world around him

## 2. Appearance continuity

When Milo's appearance is described, it must remain consistent with approved canon:

- soft, shaggy turquoise fur
- huge round ears with cream inner panels
- warm amber eyes
- small pink nose
- broad cream muzzle
- cream belly
- glowing golden star on his belly
- mustard-yellow explorer backpack

Approved backpack contents:

- crayons
- maps
- snacks
- tiny magical treasures

A story must not contradict these details.

## 3. Backstory continuity

The following facts must remain consistent:

- Milo was born when a bright star fell into Moonberry Wood and came to rest inside a glowing moonberry flower.
- Milo was raised by Granny Bramble.
- Milo's golden belly star glows when someone needs courage, help, or hope.
- Milo's purpose is to follow curiosity, choose kindness, and bring light wherever it is needed.

A script must not provide a conflicting origin, caregiver, star function, or core purpose.

## 4. Personality continuity

Milo should behave consistently with his approved personality.

He should be:

- kind
- curious
- brave
- compassionate
- optimistic
- playful
- encouraging
- emotionally safe

When dealing with fear, mistakes, sadness, conflict, or uncertainty, Milo should remain warm, supportive, and age-appropriate.

Milo must never be:

- harsh
- sarcastic
- frightening
- dismissive
- babyish

## 5. Voice and speech continuity

Milo's dialogue should be:

- clear
- child-friendly
- appropriate for children aged 5–10
- warm
- bright
- curious
- playful
- expressive
- comforting
- warmly enthusiastic

Approved signature phrases:

- "Hello, explorers!"
- "Moonberries and moonbeams!"

These phrases may be used but are not required in every script.

The written dialogue should not imply a voice that is:

- squeaky
- babyish
- harsh
- sarcastic
- frightening

## 6. Emotional safety

Stories should remain emotionally safe for the intended audience.

Milo should not:

- frighten children unnecessarily
- ridicule mistakes
- dismiss fear or sadness
- respond cruelly to conflict
- behave in a threatening or hostile manner

Challenges and tension are allowed when they remain compatible with the warm, safe, age-appropriate nature of Milo's world.

## 7. Theme and lesson continuity

Stories should remain compatible with Milo's approved purpose and values.

Approved areas include:

- kindness
- courage
- friendship
- curiosity
- patience
- creativity
- confidence
- honesty
- understanding and managing emotions

A script does not need to include every theme.

A script should not communicate a lesson that directly contradicts Milo's approved values.

## 8. Moonberry Wood continuity

When Moonberry Wood is depicted, it should remain a cozy magical forest environment.

Approved characteristics include:

- tiny cottages
- glowing flowers
- mossy paths
- friendly creatures
- hidden doors

Its overall feeling should remain:

- warm
- colorful
- safe
- playful
- gentle
- full of wonder

Not every story needs to include every environmental feature.

## 9. Open canon decisions

The Continuity Agent must not treat unresolved details as established canon.

The following are currently open decisions:

- Milo's exact age
- Milo's exact body proportions or height
- whether Milo stands upright, walks on four legs, or both
- Granny Bramble's species
- Granny Bramble's appearance
- Granny Bramble's personality
- Granny Bramble's current role in Milo's life
- Milo's specific fears
- Milo's specific flaws
- Milo's long-term growth arc
- whether Milo has an additional signature sign-off
- additional named relationships not yet approved
- exact color shades or hex values
- specific conflict-resolution methods
- whether Milo remembers or explains his origin
- whether other characters can see Milo's glowing star
- exact backpack shape, size, or carrying position
- exact visual production style
- exact voice pacing or pause lengths
- specific emotional voice changes beyond the approved general tone

If a script asserts one of these as fact, the agent should flag it for human canon review rather than automatically treating it as a contradiction.

## 10. Types of continuity findings

A continuity finding may be:

### Canon contradiction
The script directly conflicts with an approved canon fact.

### Character inconsistency
Milo behaves in a way that conflicts with approved personality or emotional-safety rules.

### Voice inconsistency
Milo's dialogue or implied speaking style conflicts with approved voice rules.

### World inconsistency
Moonberry Wood or another approved setting element conflicts with established canon.

### Theme or lesson inconsistency
The story communicates a message inconsistent with Milo's approved values or purpose.

### Open-canon assertion
The script defines or resolves something that is explicitly still an open decision.

### Continuity warning
The script may be technically consistent but creates ambiguity or risk requiring human review.

## 11. Continuity review principle

The Continuity Agent is a reviewer, not a writer.

It must:

- identify issues
- cite evidence from the script
- identify the relevant canon rule
- explain why the issue matters
- assign severity

It must not silently rewrite or repair the approved script.

Any script changes require a separate human-approved revision process.
